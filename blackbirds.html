Blackbirds of Iowa

Six dark, icterid “blackbirds” are regular in Iowa, and in a big mixed flock they blur together. Start with three structural marks that don’t change with season: the eye, the bill and the tail. Then use the tab that fits the bird: a black male in spring or summer, a brown or gray female or young bird, or a fall–winter bird with rusty or buff edges. Try the Blackbird Picker to work through a bird in the field.

Who to expect

All six are “Regular” on the Iowa Ornithologists’ Union checklist (seen in at least 7 of the last 10 years).

  • Red-winged BlackbirdRegular; nestsMarshes, wet ditches and fields statewide; males sing from cattails and fence wires from early spring. A few wintercheck.
  • Common GrackleRegular; nestsTowns, farmyards, lawns and fields. Iowa’s birds are the “bronzed” form: blue head, bronze body. A few winter.
  • Brown-headed CowbirdRegular; nestsFields, pastures and woodland edges. Lays its eggs in other birds’ nests, so young cowbirds are raised by warblers, sparrows and others.
  • Great-tailed GrackleRegular; nestsFirst nested in Iowa in 1983 at Riverton, Fremont County, after spreading north from Texas, and has kept spreading. Most often near water and in towns, mainly in the west and southcheck.
  • Rusty BlackbirdRegular; migrantDoesn’t nest here. Extreme dates run 6 Sep to 29 Apr. Wet woods, swamps and flooded bottoms, often walking in shallow water and flipping leaves.
  • Brewer’s BlackbirdRegular; migrantDoesn’t nest here. Extreme dates run 26 Sep to 11 May. Open fields, feedlots and short grass; walks with a head-bob.
  • Look-alikesNot coveredEuropean Starling: short, square tail, triangular wings, and a yellow bill in spring or white spots in winter. Yellow-headed Blackbird: males have a yellow head and white wing patch; females are brown with a yellowish throat and breast.

Step 1: Eye, bill and tail

Tap a check to see where to look. These marks hold for both sexes and all seasons.

All three work in any season; together they narrow most birds to one or two species.

Listen too. Red-winged sings a gurgling “conk-la-ree!”; cowbird a liquid gurgle ending in a thin whistle; Common Grackle a harsh, squeaky “rusty gate”; Great-tailed loud whistles, clacks and rattles. Rusty’s creaky “rusty hinge” and Brewer’s short, wheezy squeak are the hardest paircheck. Rough lengths: cowbird 16–22 cm, Red-winged 17–23, Rusty and Brewer’s 21–25, Common Grackle 28–34, Great-tailed 38–46check.

Step 2: Identify it

Black males (spring and summer)

All six males are black, or black with a brown head. Gloss colors show only in good light.

Pitfalls

  • Gloss depends on light.In shade or backlight every male looks plain black. Wait for sun, or use eye, bill and tail.
  • Rusty and Brewer’s males in spring.Both have pale eyes and slim bills. Brewer’s is glossier, with a purple head; Rusty is duller and may keep a few rusty edges into April.
  • Molting grackles.In late summer grackles can drop their tails and look short-tailed. Use the heavy bill and pale eye.

Brown or gray birds (females and young, spring and summer)

Females are where most mistakes happen. Body color and pattern come first, then the eye and bill.

Pitfalls

  • Female Red-winged vs. sparrows.She is bigger than most sparrows, with a long, sharp bill instead of a short cone.
  • Young cowbirds.A streaky gray-brown fledgling begging from a much smaller bird, like a warbler or sparrow, is a young cowbird.
  • Female Brewer’s vs. cowbird.Both are plain and dark-eyed. Brewer’s is bigger and darker, with a longer, slimmer bill.
  • Some female Brewer’s have pale eyes.Don’t call a pale-eyed gray female a Rusty on the eye alone.

Fall and winter (both sexes)

From about October, fresh feathers have rusty or buff tips that wear off by spring. This is when Rusty is easiest.

Pitfalls

  • Edges wear off.By April, Rusty’s rust is mostly gone, and spring birds need the spring marks.
  • Young male Brewer’s.Some show buff tips in their first winter. Check the tertials and the purple head.
  • Mixed flocks.Winter flocks can mix all six with starlings. Go bird by bird.

Iowa timing

  • Rusty: migrant; extreme dates 6 Sep (2003) to 29 Apr. A few wintercheck.
  • Brewer’s: migrant; extreme dates 26 Sep (2012) to 11 May (1996).
  • Red-winged, Common Grackle, cowbird, Great-tailed: nest here, spring through summer. Most leave for the winter; a few of each stay.
  • June through August: a Rusty or Brewer’s would be far outside its known dates.

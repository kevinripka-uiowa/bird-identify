    Notes and sources

    • Use several marks. Every mark has exceptions. James rates five most useful: tail flicking, breast barring, a pale base to the lower mandible, black or white on the forehead over the bill, and white behind the eye above the mask. A white crescent under the eye also points to Northern.
    • Watch the season. Both can show some barring and a pale bill base in fall and early winter, and by late winter both can lose the barring and darken the bill.
    • Iowa. Loggerhead nests in scattered grassland, pasture and hedgerow country and is listed as a Species of Greatest Conservation Need and of special concern. Northern Shrike is a winter visitor; Iowa’s extreme dates run from 1 October to 13 April.

    Sources: R. D. James, “Field Identification of Shrikes,” Ontario Birds 1:18–21 (1983); Nemecek and Mutchler, “Comments on Winter Shrike Identification in Missouri,” The Bluebird (2019); Sharon Stiteler, Learn to Tell a Northern Shrike From a Loggerhead Shrike (Audubon); Iowa Ornithologists’ Union checklist and early and late dates; Iowa DNR, Iowa Wildlife Action Plan (2025).

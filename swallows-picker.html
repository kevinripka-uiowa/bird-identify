    Notes and sources

    • Look for contrast, not color. A swallow zipping past gives you a second or two. Dark against pale (a dark throat, a dark band, a pale rump) is what you can actually see.
    • Tail shape is the quickest mark. Long streamers: Barn. Pointed outer feathers on a big, gliding bird: Purple Martin. A clear fork: Tree. Square or barely notched: Cliff, Rough-winged or Bank. Spread tails lose their fork, so judge a closed tail.
    • Watch one bird for a while. Flight style varies from bird to bird; compare it with a swallow you already know.
    • Not a swallow: Chimney Swift is a dark “flying cigar” with a stubby tail and stiff, flickering wingbeats, and it never perches on wires.

    Sources: Woody Goss, Chicago Bird Alliance, Identification of Swallows in Flight (2023); USGS Patuxent ID accounts for Purple Martin, Tree, Northern Rough-winged, Bank, Cliff and Barn; All About Birds species comparisons; Iowa Ornithologists’ Union checklist; Iowa Bird Life field reports, spring 2005 and fall 2005.

Brown Thrushes

Wood Thrush and the four Catharus thrushes, Veery, Gray-cheeked, Swainson’s and Hermit, are brown above and spotted below. Check the date first, then the face (eye-ring and cheeks), the color above and whether the tail matches the back, then the spots and flanks. Song settles most. Try the Thrush Picker to work through a bird in the field.

Who to expect

Status is approximate.check

  • Wood ThrushRegular; nestsNests in mature deciduous woods, mostly in eastern Iowa. Back from late April; tracked Iowa birds started returning in April 2025.
  • Swainson’s ThrushCommon migrantUsually the commonest migrant thrush, peaking in mid-May and again in September.
  • Hermit ThrushCommon migrantThe early-spring (April) and late-fall (October) thrush. The only one of these likely in winter.
  • Gray-cheeked ThrushUncommon migrantMid- to late May and September into October, often outnumbered by Swainson’s.
  • VeeryUncommon migrantMostly May and late August–September; may nest very locally in the northeast.

Voice, behavior and timing

  • Songs.Veery’s spiral down; Swainson’s spiral up. Hermit starts each phrase with a long, clear note, which the others lack. Wood sings ee-oh-lay and ends with a buzzy trill. Gray-cheeked is thinner and more nasal, dropping at the end.check
  • Calls.Veery: a low, down-slurred veer. Swainson’s: a liquid whit. Hermit: a low chuck. Wood: rapid pit-pit-pit. Gray-cheeked: a nasal vee-a, higher than Veery’s. Night flight calls differ too: Swainson’s usually rise, Hermit’s fall.check
  • Hermit’s tail.Hermit slowly raises its tail and lowers it, often flicking its wings. The others don’t.check
  • Date.From November to March, Hermit is the only one of these likely in the U.S. Hermit also passes earliest in spring (April) and latest in fall (October).

Pitfalls

  • Lighting.Warm evening light makes a Swainson’s or Gray-cheeked look like a Veery. Check the face and flanks.
  • Gray-cheeked vs. Swainson’s.Both are dull above with dark spots. Swainson’s spectacles and buffy face are the difference; Gray-cheeked looks plain-faced.
  • Variable birds.Western and northern populations look different from typical eastern birds, so weigh several marks.
  • Bicknell’s Thrush.A Gray-cheeked look-alike of northeastern mountains, not expected in Iowa.

Iowa timing

Rough windowscheck:

  • Hermit Thrush: late March to early May, peaking in April; late September to November, peaking in mid-October.
  • Wood Thrush: late April to September; Iowa Motus stations first detected a returning tagged bird on 29 April 2025.
  • Swainson’s Thrush: early May to early June, peaking about mid-May; late August to early October.
  • Gray-cheeked Thrush: about 7 May to early June; September to mid-October.
  • Veery: May; late August to September.

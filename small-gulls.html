Iowa Small and Hooded Gulls

Franklin’s and Bonaparte’s are the small gulls you’ll see most. The rest are rarities that usually turn up in their flocks. Age the bird, then let the wing pattern do most of the work.

Who to expect

Status follows the Iowa Ornithologists’ Union; check eBird for your county. Check every Franklin’s and Bonaparte’s flock: that’s where the rarities hide.

  • Franklin’sCommonSpring mostly April; big fall flocks Sept–Nov, a few into December. Missouri River oxbows, reservoirs, plowed fields. Adult in 2 years.check
  • Bonaparte’sUncommon to fairly commonApril–early May; late Oct–early Dec, peaking near freeze-up. Reservoirs and the Mississippi. Adult in 2 years.
  • LaughingRare but regularApril–September, usually single birds with Franklin’s. Red Rock most often. Adult in 3 years.
  • Sabine’sRare but regularSeptember–early October, nearly all juveniles. Recent: Big Creek and Red Rock, Sept 2025. Adult in 2 years.
  • Black-legged KittiwakeRare but regularNot every year. November–December, almost always juveniles. Red Rock, Saylorville, Mississippi dams. Adult in about 3 years.check
  • LittleCasual26 records.check April and Oct–Nov, usually with Bonaparte’s. Adult in 2–3 years.
  • Black-headedAccidental17 records, often with Franklin’s or Bonaparte’s;check several in summer at the Iowa Great Lakes. Adult in 2 years.
  • Ross’sAccidental4 records, including Red Rock in Oct 1993 (the first) and an adult there in Dec 1997.check Adult in 2 years.

Iowa also has 2 records of Ivory Gull and 1 of Heermann’s Gull. Neither is covered here.

Step 1: Age it

The wing and tail tell you the age; the head mostly tells you the season. Tap a check to see where to look. The diagram is a bird flying away from you: its left wing is shown from above, its right wing from below.

Juveniles count as 1st cycle. A brown-backed bird from July to September is a juvenile; use the 1st-cycle tab. Most small gulls look adult by their 2nd cycle. Laughing and Little can show leftover black in the wing coverts or wingtip at that age, and Franklin’s shows less white in its wingtip. Franklin’s is the only gull that replaces all its flight feathers twice a year, so spring birds look unusually fresh; don’t age it on freshness alone.

Step 2: Identify it for its age and season

In Iowa, hoods usually mean April–May and white winter heads mean August–November, so pick the tab that matches when you saw it.

Pitfalls

  • Three birds show a “W”.A 1st-cycle Little Gull, Kittiwake and Ross’s all have one. Little is the smallest and has a dark cap and square tail; Kittiwake has a black neck collar and clean gray back; Ross’s has a wedge-shaped tail.
  • Don’t call a Sabine’s by elimination.A distant juvenile Kittiwake’s white inner wing can look like Sabine’s white triangle. Confirm no dark bar on the inner wing, no neck collar and a brown, scaly back.
  • Franklin’s vs. Laughing.Use the tail band (partial vs. complete) and the breast (white vs. gray). Bill size helps, but small Laughings overlap.
  • Bonaparte’s vs. Black-headed.Check the bill first: black at every age on Bonaparte’s, two-toned on a young Black-headed. Leg color overlaps, so don’t use it.

Pitfalls

  • Brown vs. black hoods are hard to judge.In poor light a Black-headed’s brown hood looks black. Check the bill color and the underwing instead.
  • Some 1st-summer birds get hoods too.Look for a leftover dark bar on the inner wing or a tail band before treating a hooded bird as an adult.
  • Fresh-looking Franklin’s.Franklin’s molts its flight feathers twice a year, so even young birds look crisp in spring. Second-cycle birds show less white in the wingtip.

Adult, fall–winter

Hoods are gone on most species, so the head pattern and bill take over. Sabine’s is the exception: fall adults usually keep their hood.

Pitfalls

  • A dark underwing alone isn’t Ross’s.Adult Little Gulls have dark underwings too, and paler ones can look like Ross’s at a distance. Ross’s needs the wedge-shaped tail and no dark cap.
  • Franklin’s vs. Laughing in fall.Franklin’s has a crisp half-hood and a white band in the wingtip; Laughing has a smudgy head and all-dark wingtips.
  • Bonaparte’s vs. Black-headed.Both have ear spots in winter. Check the bill (black vs. red) and the underside of the outer wing (white vs. dark).

Fall Bay-breasted, Blackpoll, and Pine Warblers

In fall all three are greenish-yellow birds with white wingbars. Feet, the back, and how much tail sticks out past the undertail coverts sort most of them; the flanks and face confirm.

How to use it

Work through the checks in order. Tap a check to see where to look on the bird.

Checks run from most to least useful.

Date helps in Iowa. All three pass mainly late August to early October, peaking in mid-September. Blackpoll is mostly gone by late September and Bay-breasted by mid-October. Pine is the only one likely from November to March, sometimes at feeders. Some birds stay “Bay-breasted/Blackpoll”; that’s a correct ID, not a failure.

Pitfalls

  • Dark-legged Blackpolls.Some Blackpolls have dark legs with yellow only on the soles. Dark legs alone don’t make it a Bay-breasted; see the soles.
  • A Bay-breasted with no bay.Many first-fall birds, especially females, show no bay at all. Use the feet, undertail color and clean breast instead.
  • Yellow isn’t bay.A Blackpoll’s yellow wash can run onto the flanks. Bay is brown or chestnut, not yellow.
  • Ruffled feathers look like streaks.A Bay-breasted with ruffled breast feathers can seem streaky. Real Blackpoll streaks are fine and blurry, on the sides of the breast.
  • Light shifts the colors.Some Blackpolls look yellow-green and some Bay-breasted look dull gray in poor light. Lean on the feet, back and tail.
  • A dull Pine isn’t a “Baypoll”.Young female Pines can be gray-brown with no yellow and can even suggest an Empidonax flycatcher. Look for the unstreaked back, thick bill and long tail.
  • Cape May is the other trap.A Pine’s pale neck patch can suggest Cape May, but Cape May is streaked below in every plumage.
  • After October, think Pine.A Bay-breasted or Blackpoll after October is very unusual in Iowa; Pine is the one that lingers or winters.

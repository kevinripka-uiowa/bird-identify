Waterthrushes

Louisiana and Northern Waterthrushes are big, brown, streaky warblers that walk along the water bobbing their tails. They look alike, so stack several marks: the eyebrow, the throat and the flanks, then song, date and habitat. Try the Waterthrush Picker to work through a bird in the field.

Who to expect

  • Louisiana WaterthrushUncommon; nestsOne of the first warblers back in spring, from late March. Nests locally along clear, flowing streams in wooded ravines, mainly in eastern Iowa, and leaves early, mostly by mid-August.check
  • Northern WaterthrushRegular migrantPasses through in May and again from August into October, at any wet, shady spot, even park ponds and puddles. Nests far to the north.check

Song, habitat and timing

  • Song is the best mark.Louisiana starts with 2–5 loud, clear whistles that slur downward, then tumbles into a jumble. Northern’s song is a loud, ringing run of quick, even notes that speeds up, something like sweet sweet sweet swee wee wee chew chew chew. Calls are both a hard, metallic chink and don’t help.
  • Habitat helps in summer.Louisiana nests along clear, flowing, gravel-bottomed streams in wooded hills. Northern prefers still water: swampy woods and bogs. In migration, Northerns turn up anywhere damp and shady, so habitat means much less then.
  • Date settles many birds.Louisiana comes through first in spring, from late March, about a week or more ahead of Northern, and leaves early. After about August 20, a waterthrush in the East is almost certainly a Northern (Jon Dunn, quoted by NH Audubon).

Pitfalls

  • White Northerns.Some Northerns are white below with a whitish eyebrow. Check the eyebrow’s shape, the throat and the flanks.
  • Spotted Louisianas.Some Louisianas have spotted throats. Weigh the eyebrow and flanks.
  • Pink legs.Northern’s legs are pinkish too, and look brighter when wet.
  • Silent fall birds.Take extra care with any bird you don’t hear sing.

Iowa timing

Rough windowscheck:

  • Louisiana Waterthrush: late March to mid-August. Arrives late March and April; nests along wooded streams; most leave by mid-August. Iowa records include birds on 2 April 1982 (Boone County) and 4–6 April 1981 (Story County).
  • Northern Waterthrush: late April to early June, peaking in May; then early August to October.

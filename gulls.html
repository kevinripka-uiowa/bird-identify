Iowa White-headed Gulls

White-headed gulls change for years before they look like adults. Age the bird first, then use the checks for that age.

Who to expect

Status is approximate; check eBird or the Iowa Ornithologists’ Union for your county. Gulls gather where there’s open water: Saylorville, Red Rock, Coralville and Rathbun reservoirs, the Mississippi River locks and dams, and Missouri River oxbows such as Lake Manawa and Carter Lake.

  • Ring-billedCommonAbundant in migration; winters where water stays open. Adult in 3 years.
  • HerringCommonMostly Oct–Apr on big rivers and reservoirs.check Adult in 4 years.
  • Lesser Black-backedRare but regularMostly fall through spring.check Adult in 4 years.
  • Iceland Thayer’s & Kumlien’sRare but regularWinter. Adult in 4 years.
  • GlaucousRare but regularWinter. Adult in 4 years.
  • Great Black-backedRare but regularWinter, mostly on the Mississippi. Adult in 4 years.
  • CaliforniaRare but regularMostly fall to early winter. Adult in 4 years.
  • Short-billed formerly Mew GullAccidental22 accepted records, mostly late fall to winter on big reservoirs and the Mississippi; recent birds at Dubuque and the Quad Cities.check Adult in 3 years.
  • Slaty-backedAccidental4 records, mostly December–February on the Mississippi; an adult at Dubuque, Dec 2025–Jan 2026.check Adult in 4 years.

Step 1: Age it

Gulls are aged by cycle, from one molt to the next, rather than by calendar year. A “1st-cycle” bird hatched last summer and keeps that look, getting worn and faded, until it starts replacing its wing feathers the next spring or summer. Tap a check to see where to look.

Ring-billed and Short-billed are 3-year gulls. They get gray backs in their first winter, look almost adult in their 2nd cycle and are adult by their 3rd. The others take four years. When aging is unclear, call it a range (“2nd–3rd cycle”); that’s normal.

Pitfalls

  • Bleached birds go pale.By spring, a worn 1st-cycle Herring can fade very pale and pass for an Iceland or Glaucous. Check the bill pattern and look for darker centers on the wing feathers.
  • Pale hybrids exist.Herring × Glaucous (“Nelson’s Gull”) can look like a 1st-cycle Glaucous but usually has darker wingtips and a less crisp bill.
  • Leucistic birds are a trap.An all-pale Herring with a Herring-shaped all-black bill is not a Glaucous. Glaucous has the crisp pink-and-black bill.
  • A “young Slaty-backed” is almost always something else.A pale brown 1st-cycle gull with Thayer’s-like striped primaries is far more likely a hybrid. Photograph it and leave it unidentified.
  • Thayer’s and Kumlien’s grade into each other.Both are Iceland Gull now. Intermediate birds occur; “Iceland Gull” is a complete ID.

Pitfalls

  • Molt is gradual.A bird in late winter can be part 1st cycle, part 2nd. Weigh the newest feathers most.
  • Gray looks different at every angle.Back shade changes with light and how the bird is tilted. Only compare birds standing side by side.

3rd cycle

Looks like a slightly scruffy adult. Identify it with the adult checks, then expect these differences. Ring-billed and Short-billed are already adult by now.

Checks run from most to least useful.

Pitfalls

  • A dirty bill isn’t a ring.A 3rd-cycle Herring with a dark band near the bill tip can suggest Ring-billed. Check size, leg color and back shade.
  • A dark bill mark doesn’t make it a California.Many 3rd-cycle gulls show red and black on the bill. California also needs a dark eye, greenish to yellowish legs and a slightly darker back.

Pitfalls

  • Short-billed vs. a small Ring-billed.Some Ring-billed have a thin bill with only a faint ring, and winter Short-billed can show a dusky ring. Judge by back shade, wingtip pattern and the broad white crescent on the tertials, not the bill alone. A bulkier, dark-backed bird with a ringed bill could be a Common or Kamchatka Gull, which have no Iowa record: photograph it.
  • Hybrids happen.Herring × Glaucous (“Nelson’s Gull”) and Herring × Lesser Black-backed both turn up. A wingtip or back shade that doesn’t fit any species may be a hybrid; leave it unidentified.
  • Winter heads are streaked.Herring, Ring-billed and especially Lesser Black-backed get dusky streaking on the head in winter; Great Black-backed stays mostly white-headed. Don’t treat streaking as an age clue.
  • Leg color fades in winter.Adult Lesser Black-backed legs can look dull or pale yellow in winter; younger birds can still have fleshy legs. A dark-backed adult with pink legs needs a closer look: Great Black-backed, Slaty-backed or a hybrid.
  • A slate back isn’t enough for Slaty-backed.Its back matches Lesser Black-backed. Look for deep pink legs, the string of pearls and a broad white trailing edge, and rule out hybrids involving Lesser or Great Black-backed.
  • Rarer gulls exist.Iowa also has a record of Glaucous-winged Gull. A bird that fits nothing here deserves photos and notes.

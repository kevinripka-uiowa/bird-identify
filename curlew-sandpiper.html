Curlew Sandpiper and Its Look-alikes

Curlew Sandpiper is an Old World shorebird with five accepted Iowa records, every one a May adult in red or patchy breeding plumage. It hides among Dunlins, and shares a white rump with Stilt and White-rumped Sandpipers. Bill shape, the rump in flight and leg length do most of the work. Try the Curlew Sandpiper Picker to work through a bird in the field.

Who to expect

  • DunlinRegular migrantSpring mid-March to late June, mostly May; fall from mid-July to December, mostly late. Most fall birds are already gray.
  • Stilt SandpiperRegular migrantSpring April to mid-June; fall from late June to early November.
  • White-rumped SandpiperRegular migrantSpring late April to early July, peaking late May, right when Curlews have turned up; fall late July to early November.
  • Curlew SandpiperAccidentalFive accepted records, all May: 10 May 1985, Nashua; 13 May 1988, Coralville Reservoir; 6–7 May 2002, Red Rock Dam; 12 May 2006, Big Wall Lake; 25 May 2012, Hawkeye WA.

Behavior and pitfalls

  • Feeding.Stilt probes straight down like a dowitcher, often with its head underwater. Curlew wades a little deeper than Dunlins. Dunlin is hunched, with rapid stitching probes.
  • Molting spring birds.A May Curlew can be blotchy red and gray; a May Dunlin can show only part of its black belly.
  • Long-billed Dunlins.Females have longer bills. Check the rump, legs and eyebrow together.
  • Hybrids.Curlew crosses with Pectoral (Cox’s Sandpiper) and rarely with Dunlin or White-rumped.
  • Fall.Iowa has no fall Curlew yet, and fall birds are easy to overlook. A scaly, buffy juvenile “Dunlin” in September deserves a second look; most Dunlins have molted to gray by the time they arrive.

Iowa timing

Extremes from the IOU early and late datescheck:

  • Dunlin: spring 18 Mar to 22 Jun; fall 11 Jul to 20 Dec.
  • Stilt: spring 1 Apr to 12 Jun; fall 23 Jun to 8 Nov.
  • White-rumped: spring 23 Apr to 4 Jul; fall 21 Jul to 7 Nov.
  • Curlew: all five records 6–25 May.

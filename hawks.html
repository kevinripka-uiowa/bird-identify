Buteos & Harrier

Iowa’s six buteos and Northern Harrier. Red-tailed alone comes in five forms and several color morphs. Confirm the shape, age the bird, then use the tabs below. Many distant or odd birds are best left as “buteo sp.”; that’s a correct ID. Cooper’s, Sharp-shinned and goshawk have their own accipiter page.

Who to expect

Status codes follow the Iowa Ornithologists’ Union checklist. Fall dates come from the Hitchcock Nature Center hawkwatch in the Loess Hills (2020–2023); spring and summer windows are approximate.check

  • Red-tailed HawkRegular; nests; all yearIowa’s commonest hawk, statewide. Fall passage peaks late October to early November.
  • Red-shouldered HawkRegular; nestsState-threatened. Bottomland forest, mainly eastern and southeastern river valleys; scarce in the west. Roughly March to October.check
  • Broad-winged HawkRegular; nestsBreeds in large forests. Fall kettles peak in late September; at Hitchcock the record day was 1,206 on 28 Sep 2023. Gone by mid-October. Almost all are light birds; rufous (intermediate) and dark morphs breed mostly in western Canada and are very rare here.
  • Swainson’s HawkRegular; nestsNests uncommonly in northern and western Iowa. Fall peak late September to early October.
  • Rough-legged HawkRegular; winterOpen farmland and grassland, late October to early April.
  • Ferruginous HawkCasualWestern Iowa, mostly late September to December; Hitchcock usually logs one a year.
  • Northern HarrierRegular; rare nesterState-endangered as a breeder. Most often seen in fall and winter over grassland and marsh.

Dark birds are their own problem. Five of Iowa’s buteos have dark morphs. If the body is chocolate to black, skip to the dark morphs tab: the tail and the underwing flight feathers matter more than the body.

Step 2: Identify it

Red-tailed Hawk and its forms

The dark bar on the front edge of the inner underwing (the patagial bar) marks a light Red-tail at any age; no other regular Iowa buteo has it. Adults have red tails; juveniles have brown, narrowly banded tails and pale eyes. Northern and Krider’s are debated as true subspecies and many birds are intergrades, so treat the forms as best fits.check

Pitfalls

  • Young Red-tails have brown tails.Until their first molt, Red-tails have brown tails with many narrow, even bands. Use the patagial bar and the pale “V” on the back.
  • Krider’s vs. a pale Eastern.Pale Easterns are common. A true Krider’s has a mostly white head and a white-based, pale tail; intergrades sit in between.
  • Harlan’s vs. dark Western.Judge the tail: whitish or gray and mottled without red points to Harlan’s; red with narrow bands points to Western. Immatures overlap heavily, and banded-tailed adults may be intergrades.
  • Northern is a tendency, not a rule.Its marks are statistical, and some Northern birds have light belly bands.

Pitfalls

  • Juvenile Red-shouldered vs. Broad-winged.Body streaking won’t separate them. Red-shouldered has pale crescents, evenly banded tail and squarer wingtips; Broad-winged has a squarish pale panel, a wider dark band at the tail tip and pointed wingtips.
  • Crescents aren’t only Red-shouldered.Some Red-tails show pale crescents too; check the patagial bar.
  • Juvenile Ferruginous vs. Krider’s.Both are very white below. Ferruginous has no patagial bar and feathered legs.

Northern Harrier

Not a buteo: Iowa’s only harrier, in its own genus. The shape and the way it hunts, low over grass and marsh, give it away before any plumage detail.

Pitfalls

  • Harriers look like buteos when soaring.Check for the white rump and the long tail.
  • Rough-legged also shows white near the tail.Its white is the base of the tail itself, ending in a broad dark band, and it has black wrist patches; the harrier’s white is a rump patch above a banded tail.

Dark morphs

Dark Western Red-tailed, Harlan’s, Swainson’s, Rough-legged and Ferruginous all occur; dark and rufous (intermediate) Broad-winged are very rare in the East. Ignore the body color and judge the tail and the flight feathers.

Iowa timing

Fall counts from the Hitchcock Nature Center hawkwatch, Pottawattamie County (counts run about 1 September to mid-December):

  • Broad-winged: 8 Sep to 9 Oct in 2023, with 1,206 on 28 Sep and 986 on 29 Sep; peak 23 Sep in 2020. Big flights need east winds at this western site.
  • Swainson’s: 7 Sep to 17 Oct in 2020 (peak 119 on 30 Sep); peak 86 on 3 Oct 2023.
  • Red-tailed: peaks 1 Nov 2020 (48) and 27 Oct 2023 (139). Season totals 819 (2020), 1,729 (2021) and 1,100 (2023).
  • Rough-legged: first 23 Oct 2020 and 5 Nov 2023; peak 26 Nov 2023.
  • Northern Harrier: peaks 26 Oct 2020 and 7 Nov 2023.
  • Ferruginous: 4 birds 27–30 Sep 2020 (a site record), 1 on 24 Sep 2021, 3 from 6 Oct to 1 Dec 2023.

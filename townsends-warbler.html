Townsend’s and Black-throated Green Warblers

Black-throated Green is a common Iowa migrant; Townsend’s, its western cousin, has six Iowa records. Both have yellow faces, black throats on adult males, two white wingbars and streaked flanks. The cheek settles most birds; then check the breast, the back and the vent. Try the Townsend’s Warbler Picker to work through a bird in the field.

Who to expect

  • Black-throated Green WarblerCommon migrantSpring from early April (record) into June, mostly late April–May; fall from early August to early November, mostly September.
  • Townsend’s WarblerAccidentalSix Iowa records. Three found: 7 May 1950, Davenport; 12 May 1988, Wildcat Den State Park; 11 May 1991, Stone State Park, Sioux City.

Voice and pitfalls

  • Songs overlap.Black-throated Green sings a lazy, buzzy zee-zee-zoo-zoo-zee. Townsend’s gives rising buzzy notes and then a second phrase; some songs sound like Black-throated Green. Don’t identify a rarity by song.
  • Calls.Townsend’s: a soft chip or tup. Black-throated Green: a sharp tsip. Similar enough not to rely on.

Pitfalls

  • Dull young Townsend’s.The cheek and crown are olive, not black, but still darker than the yellow around them, and the breast is still yellow.check
  • Young or female Blackburnian.Also has a dark cheek framed in yellow and pale back stripes. Its face is more orange, the throat often blackish on males, and the crown has a pale center. Iowa’s 1991 Townsend’s observer ruled it out by color.
  • Hybrids.Townsend’s hybridizes with Hermit Warbler in the Pacific Northwest and with Black-throated Green where they meet in the Rockies. Hermit has no Iowa record.

Iowa timing

  • Black-throated Green: spring earliest 1, 8 and 13 Apr, latest 19 Jun, 3 Jul and 3 Jul; fall earliest 2, 11 and 13 Aug, latest 28 Oct, 3 Nov and 10 Nov (IOU early and late dates).
  • Townsend’s: the three records found fall 7–12 May. The other three weren’t found, so a fall pattern is unknown.

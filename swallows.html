Swallows of Iowa

Six swallows nest in Iowa, and most of the time you see them overhead for a second or two. Learn the tail shapes first, then look for contrast rather than color: a dark throat, a dark band, a pale rump. Age matters in late summer, when brown juveniles fill the wires. Try the Swallow Picker to work through a bird in the field.

Who to expect

Status codes follow the Iowa Ornithologists’ Union checklist. All six swallows are regular and nest here.

  • Barn SwallowRegular; nestsFarms, bridges and open country statewide. Builds an open mud cup inside barns and sheds.
  • Tree SwallowRegular; nestsThe first back in spring (mid-March) and the last to leave; thousands gather at reservoirs in September and October. Nest boxes and tree holes near water.
  • Cliff SwallowRegular; nestsColonies of gourd-shaped mud nests under bridges, dams and eaves.
  • Purple MartinRegular; nestsNests almost only in martin houses and gourds. Gathers in big late-summer roosts.
  • Northern Rough-winged SwallowRegular; nestsPairs nest singly in holes in banks, pipes and bridges, often along rivers and creeks.
  • Bank SwallowRegular; nestsColonies of burrows in sand and gravel pits and river cutbanks.
  • Chimney SwiftLook-alikeNot a swallow. A sooty “flying cigar” with a stubby tail and stiff, flickering wingbeats, chittering overhead in towns; it never perches on wires.
  • Cave and Violet-green SwallowsAccidentalCave Swallow has one accepted Iowa record. Both would need full documentation.

Step 2: Identify it

Adults

Barn, adult male Purple Martin and Tree are distinctive. Bank, Cliff, Rough-winged and female-type martins take more care: look for the contrast between the throat and the chest.

Pitfalls

  • Barn Swallows can look pale below.Some adults are pale buff, not orange. Use the streamers.
  • Cliff vs. Rough-winged from below.Both have square tails and dark throats. Cliff’s throat ends sharply against a pale chest; Rough-winged’s blends in. Then wait for Cliff’s pale rump.
  • Female martins vs. smaller swallows.Their patterns are like Rough-winged or Cliff. Size, the pointed outer tail feathers and the gliding flight give them away.
  • Molting tails.In late summer, adults molt and streamers can be short or missing.

Juveniles (June into fall)

Young birds are duller and browner, with blurry patterns and shorter tails. The biggest trap is a brown young Tree Swallow, which can look like a Bank or Rough-winged.check

Pitfalls

  • Young Tree vs. Bank.A Tree’s band is weak and blurry; a Bank’s is solid and dark. Tree is also bigger, with a back that doesn’t contrast with its rump.
  • Young Barn without streamers.The fork is still deeper than any other swallow’s, and the throat is rusty-buff.
  • Mixed flocks on wires.Late-summer flocks mix every species and age. Compare birds side by side: Bank is obviously the smallest.

Iowa timing

Rough windowscheck, with dates from the 2005 Iowa Bird Life field reports:

  • Tree Swallow: mid-March to October. First 16 Mar 2005 (ties the second-earliest record); 7,000 at Saylorville Reservoir on 11 Sep; last 30 Oct.
  • Purple Martin: early April to about mid-September. First 2 Apr 2005; 1,062 roosting at Jester Park, Polk County, in late August.
  • Northern Rough-winged Swallow: April to August. First 9 Apr 2005; last reported 13 Aug.
  • Bank Swallow: mid-April to August. First 19 Apr 2005; 1,500 along the Des Moines River on 7 Aug; last reported 13 Aug.
  • Cliff Swallow: mid-April to early October. First 19 Apr 2005; last 9 Oct.
  • Barn Swallow: April to October. First 10 Apr 2005; 2,346 passed Saylorville in two hours on 1 Oct; last 28 Oct.

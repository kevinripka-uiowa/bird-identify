Mourning, Connecticut and MacGillivray’s Warblers

Three skulking, gray-hooded warblers that are yellow below, plus the two common birds most often mistaken for them: Nashville Warbler and a dull Common Yellowthroat. MacGillivray’s has only one accepted Iowa record, so it’s the one to rule in carefully. Check the eye-ring first, then the throat and hood, then how far the undertail coverts reach. Try the Mourning-type Warbler Picker to work through a bird in the field.

Who to expect

Status from the IOU state checklist; dates are Iowa’s record early and late spring dates.

  • Nashville WarblerCommon migrantSpring 14 Apr–13 Jun at the extremes; also common in fall. Gray head, white eye-ring, but a yellow throat.
  • Connecticut WarblerUncommon migrantA late spring migrant: 6 May–16 Jun at the extremes, mostly late May. Also passes in fall.
  • Mourning WarblerRegular migrantSpring 2 May–20 Jun at the extremes; common in early September in western Iowa.
  • Common YellowthroatCommon; nestsFrom mid-April; stragglers into winter. Females and young are the look-alikes.
  • MacGillivray’s WarblerAccidentalOne accepted record: an adult male, 2 Jun 1995, Eagle City County Park, Hardin Co. A fall 1997 bird in Ida Co. wasn’t accepted.

Voice, behavior and timing

  • Chips.Connecticut: a squeaky, musical pwik, mostly at dawn in fall. Mourning: a louder, huskier pwik, much like a catbird note. MacGillivray’s: a sharper tik. Nashville: a high, thin seetcheck. Yellowthroat: a soft jip or a rough chatter.
  • Gait.Connecticut walks on the ground and along branches; the rest hop.
  • Nashville’s tail.Nashville flicks its tail, but steady tail-wagging belongs mainly to the western form. Palm Warbler is the true tail-pumper.
  • Season.Connecticut is one of the last spring migrants, mostly late May into June. Mourning overlaps it. Nashville arrives weeks earlier.

Pitfalls

  • Young Mourning with a near-complete ring.An August 2016 bird at Jester Park, Polk Co., with a prominent eye-ring was resolved as a Mourning: short undertail coverts ruled out Connecticut. Mourning rings are thinner and usually broken.
  • Fall MacGillivray’s.Iowa’s committee has said a fall female or young bird likely needs measurements in the hand. MacGillivray’s has a longer tail: flat wing minus tail is 2–13 mm vs. Mourning’s 9–18 mm (Pyle), and the ranges overlap. The two also hybridize.
  • Dull yellowthroats.Mourning is bigger, with a heftier pinkish bill and more even yellow below. A yellowthroat’s belly is whitish, with brownish flanks.
  • Nashville vs. a young Mourning.Both can show a yellowish throat. Nashville is smaller and sharper-billed, with a complete ring, a white patch near the legs and a grayer back.

Other look-alikes

  • Orange-crowned Warbler.Moderately cited, mainly against Nashville and dull young birds: faint broken eye-ring, dark eyeline, blurry breast streaks, brightest yellow under the tail.
  • Kentucky, Canada and dull Yellow Warblers.Only weakly cited. Kentucky has a black mask and yellow spectacles; Canada a blue-gray back and a necklace; a dull Yellow has yellow tail spots.

Iowa timing

Spring extremes from the IOU early and late dates (three earliest and three latest)check:

  • Nashville: earliest 14, 17 and 20 Apr; latest 5, 5 and 13 Jun.
  • Connecticut: earliest 6, 6 and 7 May; latest 8, 10 and 16 Jun.
  • Mourning: earliest 2, 2 and 8 May; latest 17, 19 and 20 Jun.
  • Common Yellowthroat: arrives 17–19 Apr at the earliest; stragglers to early January.
  • MacGillivray’s: the one accepted record is 2 Jun 1995.

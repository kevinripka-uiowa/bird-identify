    Notes and sources

    • Only two marks are close to certain. White feather bases on the neck (Chihuahuan) and nasal bristles reaching more than halfway along the bill (Chihuahuan) both need a close look. Voice, size and place only shift the odds.
    • Crow or raven first. A fan-shaped tail, a “caw”, steady rowing flight and a slimmer bill all point to American Crow.
    • Texas. Both ravens live here all year. Common Ravens hold to the mountains and canyons of the Trans-Pecos and the cliffs of the Edwards Plateau; Chihuahuan Ravens use flatter, drier country from the Panhandle through west Texas to the South Texas brush. They overlap in places such as Big Bend and the Edwards Plateau.

    Sources: All About Birds, Chihuahuan Raven compared; Audubon, Chihuahuan Raven and How to tell a raven from a crow; Pieplow and Spencer, nasal bristle length in Common and Chihuahuan Ravens (Western Birds); Nathan Pieplow, Common vs. Chihuahuan Ravens (voice); Texas Breeding Bird Atlas: Chihuahuan Raven; Texas Parks & Wildlife magazine; eBird.

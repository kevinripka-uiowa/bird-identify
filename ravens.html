Chihuahuan and Common Ravens

For trips to Texas: status and places on this page are for Texas, not Iowa. Rule out American Crow first, then look for the two marks that actually separate the ravens: white feather bases on the neck and long nasal bristles, both Chihuahuan. Everything else shifts the odds. eBird’s advice is that many ravens are best left as “raven sp.”, and that’s a correct ID. Try the Raven Picker to work through a bird in the field.

Who to expect in Texas

  • Chihuahuan RavenResident; nestsFlat, arid, open country: the Panhandle and High Plains, the Trans-Pecos lowlands, the western Edwards Plateau, South Texas brush country and along the Rio Grandecheck. Nests on utility poles, windmills and lone trees; egg dates in the Texas Breeding Bird Atlas run 11 March to 3 July. Gathers in big flocks after nesting, and some winter away from breeding areas.
  • Common RavenResident; nestsMountains, canyons and cliffs: the Trans-Pecos ranges (Guadalupe, Davis, Chisos and Big Bend) and the Edwards Plateaucheck. Usually in pairs that hold territories all year, though it flocks at dumps.
  • American CrowResident; nestsTowns, woods and farmland, mainly in east and central Texas; scarce in the arid westcheck.
  • Fish CrowEast Texas onlyThe upper coast east of Houston and up the Sabine and Red Rivers, spreading west along rivers and lakes. A nasal “uh-uh” call. Not a raven confusion; it lives outside both ravens’ ranges.
  • Tamaulipas CrowCasualOnce regular in winter at the Brownsville landfill; last known nesting in 2001. Now very rare in the Lower Rio Grande Valley. Small, glossy, with a croaking call.

Pitfalls

  • No white isn’t a Common Raven.Chihuahuan’s white is hidden unless the feathers are ruffled.
  • Bristles are hard to judge.Angle and light change how far they seem to reach. Use a sharp side-on photo.
  • Bill size overlaps.Male Chihuahuans and female Commons come close; use it only with other marks.

Far off or flying

At a distance, nothing is certain. Put together the place, the voice, the size and the flock, and expect to call some birds “raven sp.”

Pitfalls

  • Pitch overlaps.Common Ravens give a huge range of calls, some as high as a Chihuahuan’s.
  • Size without a yardstick.A lone raven overhead is hard to size. Compare with a nearby hawk, vulture or crow.
  • Winter wanderers.Some Chihuahuans winter away from breeding areas, so a flock can turn up in unexpected places.

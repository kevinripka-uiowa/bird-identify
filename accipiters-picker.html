    Notes and sources

    • Combine marks. No single mark is safe. HawkWatch says separating Sharp-shinned from Cooper’s takes years, and size misleads, especially in flight.
    • Tail shape wears. A folded square or rounded tail is most useful in fall. In spring, worn or molting tails change shape, and a spread Sharp-shinned tail can look round.
    • Eye color by age. Juveniles have yellow (Cooper’s yellow-green) eyes, turning orange and then red over several years, so eye color doesn’t always match plumage.
    • Iowa status (Iowa Ornithologists’ Union checklist). Cooper’s nests statewide, towns included, all year. Sharp-shinned is mainly a migrant and winter bird and a rare nester; its fall peak is late September to mid-October. American Goshawk is rare, from mid-October into winter, with more in invasion years.
    • Names. North American goshawks were split from the Eurasian bird in 2023 as American Goshawk; in 2025 it and Cooper’s moved to the genus Astur, leaving Sharp-shinned in Accipiter.

    Sources: HawkWatch International: how to tell accipiters apart; HawkWatch fact sheets for Sharp-shinned, Cooper’s and American Goshawk; Hitchcock Nature Center hawkwatch reports; Iowa Ornithologists’ Union checklist; Audubon on the 2025 name changes.

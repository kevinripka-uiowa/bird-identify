Iowa Junco Decoder

Most Iowa juncos are Slate-colored, but not every non-Oregon bird is: Cassiar-type birds (Slate-colored × Oregon intergrades) are reported regularly in Iowa.

How to use it

Work through three checks, in this order, on any odd junco. Tap a check to see where to look on the bird.

Adult males are the easiest to sort. Females and first-winter birds are browner and duller in every form, so many can only be called “Dark-eyed Junco”. That’s a correct ID, not a failure.

Pitfalls

  • Brown Slate-colored females look Oregon-ish.Check the hood border: a smudgy, concave edge that bleeds into the flanks points to Slate-colored; a crisp, convex edge points to Oregon.
  • Cassiar is a sliding scale, not a clean form.It is widely considered a Slate-colored × Oregon hybrid swarm, so expect every in-between. eBird has no separate entry for a generic intergrade; “Dark-eyed Junco (cismontanus)” is the closest.
  • One wingbar isn’t White-winged.Some Slate-colored birds show thin white wingbars. Require pale overall gray, larger size and extra white in the tail too.
  • Light changes everything.Gray can look brown in low or warm light. Compare birds side by side in the same flock before calling one odd.
  • Juveniles are streaky.In summer, a streaked bird with white outer tail feathers is a young junco; it can’t be assigned to a group.

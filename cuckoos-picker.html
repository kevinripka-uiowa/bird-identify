    Notes and sources

    • No bill? Use the wing and the tail. A rufous flash in the outer wing means Yellow-billed at any age; Black-billed’s wing is plain. Underneath, Yellow-billed’s tail is black with big white ovals; Black-billed’s is gray with thin white tips.
    • Young birds: young Yellow-billeds can have all-dark bills and smaller tail spots, so the wing matters even more. Young Black-billeds have a buff or yellowish eye-ring, not red.
    • Voice overlaps. Even-pitched “cu-cu-cu” groups mean Black-billed; a slowing, knocking series is usually Yellow-billed, but Black-billed’s long call is similar. It’s fine to leave a heard-only bird as “cuckoo sp.”
    • Iowa: both nest statewide from May to September. Earliest records are 23–24 April; Black-billed’s latest are 17–25 October, Yellow-billed’s 30 October to 4 November.

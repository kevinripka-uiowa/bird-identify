Tropical and Couch’s Kingbirds

Two yellow-bellied kingbirds of south Texas that were treated as one species until 1983. Voice always settles it. Without voice, many birds can still be named from a combination of marks: the primaries on the folded wing, the bill, the wingtip length, the chest and the tail. Each mark overlaps, so never use one alone. Try the Kingbird Picker to work through a bird in the field.

Who to expect

Status from the Texas Breeding Bird Atlas and Lee (2022).

  • Couch’s Kingbird Tyrannus couchiiCommon; nestsThe usual one across south Texas, from Del Rio through San Antonio to Victoria and the Valley, now locally north to Austin and Houston. Some leave in winter; others spread north and east along the coast. Numbers rise from mid-March to early April.
  • Tropical Kingbird Tyrannus melancholicusUncommon, local; nestsPresent all year in the Lower Rio Grande Valley (Brownsville, Hidalgo County). Has nested at Cottonwood Campground in Big Bend since 1997. Wanders north to the Edwards Plateau and upper coast in spring and summer.

First, rule out Western and Cassin’s. Tropical and Couch’s have yellow right up to the top of the chest, a greenish back, a dark ear patch, and a longer, dark brown, often notched tail. Western and Cassin’s have gray chests, black square tails and longer wingtips. Western has white outer tail feathers; Cassin’s has a very dark gray head and cheek. Backlit pale tail-feather edges on Tropical or Couch’s can look like Western’s white sides.

Voice and habitat

  • Tropical’s voice.Calls and songs are a high, drawn-out twitter, also described as a rapid, high staccato. Often rendered pip-pip-pip-pip.
  • Couch’s voice.A distinctive burry brrREEeeeer that rises, then falls in a long, drawn-out tail, given singly or a few in a row. Also short, isolated pip notes, lower than Tropical’s twitter. The song is 2–5 short puwee phrases, then a rapid string of high pips and twitters.
  • Record it.Both call all year. A phone recording settles a bird that the marks leave open.
  • Habitat.Tropical prefers more open country with scattered trees and perches high, often on phone wires in towns and along roads. Couch’s prefers brushier woodland and thorn scrub. Both are often near water. A tendency only.

Pitfalls

  • Worn summer birds.Adults look frayed from June to August. Wear can erase the pale primary edges of Tropical, or make Couch’s dark primaries look pale-edged. Lean on voice, bill and tail then. Feathers are fresh again after the molt, by late fall.
  • Light on the wing.A shaded wing can make Tropical’s primaries look dark; bright sun can make plain edges shine. Look from several angles.
  • No single mark.Bill, wingtip, chest, tail and shape all overlap. Name a silent bird only when several agree, and leave some unidentified.
  • Vagrant Tropicals from South America.The southern subspecies has a longer wingtip and a more forked tail. That matters mostly for strays far from Texas.
  • Back color.Couch’s is said to be greener-backed and Tropical grayer, but this is hard to see and light changes it.check

Where and when in Texas

  • Lower Rio Grande Valley: both, all year. Couch’s is far commoner and returns in numbers from mid-March to early April. Couch’s nests from early April to late August, mostly from May.
  • Big Bend: Tropical, which has nested at Cottonwood Campground since 1997. Couch’s is very rare there.check
  • Rest of south Texas, Austin, Houston: mostly Couch’s. Tropical wanders north to the Edwards Plateau and the upper coast in spring and summer and sometimes nests.
  • Barrier islands: Couch’s tends to be rare on the islands along the immediate coast.
  • Winter: in some winters many Couch’s leave south Texas; in others numbers rise and small flocks appear. Some spread north and east along the coast to Louisiana.

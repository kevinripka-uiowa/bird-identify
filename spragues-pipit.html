Sprague’s Pipit and Its Look-alikes

Sprague’s Pipit is a secretive bird of dry native grass, with 15 accepted Iowa records since 1998, nearly all in late April to mid-May or late September to October. It’s easy to dismiss as an American Pipit, or to flush and lose as a Vesper Sparrow. How it flushes, the legs and the back do most of the work. Try the Sprague’s Pipit Picker to work through a bird in the field.

Who to expect

  • American PipitCommon migrantSpring early March to late May; fall mid-August to January. Flocks on plowed fields, mudflats and shores.
  • Vesper SparrowCommonBreeds; from early March to fall, a few into winter. Roadsides, field edges and bare soil.
  • Sprague’s PipitAccidental15 accepted records since 1998: spring 19 Apr to 16 May, fall 24 Sep to 28 Oct, plus one on 22 Feb 1998. Earlier reports go back to 1895.

Behavior and pitfalls

  • Where.Sprague’s wants dry short grass: native prairie, pastures, hayfields. It avoids brush and plowed fields, which is where American Pipits usually are.
  • Flushing.Most Iowa Sprague’s were found by walking grass and watching what flushed. Mark where it lands; it may sit tight on the second flush.
  • Horned Lark.It also flocks on bare fields, but it has black face and breast marks and a black tail.
  • Lapland Longspur.In flocks on plowed and stubble fields, with chestnut in the wing and a rattling call.
  • Older records.Iowa has about 19 Sprague’s reports from 1895 to 1994 that aren’t in the modern accepted list.

Iowa timing

  • American Pipit: spring 2 Mar to 2 Jun; fall 12 Aug to 25 Jan.
  • Vesper Sparrow: from 7 Mar; a few linger into January.
  • Sprague’s Pipit: spring 19 Apr to 16 May; fall 24 Sep to 28 Oct.

To check

  • Bill, lores and breast band.Sprague’s pale bill with a dark ridge, pale lores and fine “necklace” are common guide marks, but none was on a page I could read.

Iowa Accipiters

Sharp-shinned Hawk, Cooper’s Hawk and American Goshawk: the bird-hunting hawks with short, rounded wings and long tails. Juveniles and birds in flight are the hard part, so start there. Combine several marks, and leave the rest as “accipiter sp.”; that’s a correct ID. Buteos and Northern Harrier are on the Buteos & Harrier page.

Who to expect

Status codes follow the Iowa Ornithologists’ Union checklist. Fall dates come from the Hitchcock Nature Center hawkwatch in the Loess Hills (2020–2023); other months are approximate.check

  • Cooper’s HawkRegular; nests; all yearStatewide, including towns and backyard feeders. The accipiter you’ll see most.
  • Sharp-shinned HawkRegular; rare nesterMainly a migrant and winter bird. Fall peak late September to mid-October.
  • American GoshawkRare; winterTwo or three a year at Hitchcock, from mid-October; more in invasion years.

Pitfalls

  • Size misleads.Females are much bigger than males. A female Sharp-shinned is close to a male Cooper’s, and a male goshawk is close to a female Cooper’s.
  • A spread Sharp-shinned tail looks round.Judge tail shape only when the tail is folded, and mostly in fall, before wear and molt.
  • Cooper’s courtship flight.In spring, Cooper’s display with slow, exaggerated, owl-like wingbeats that look nothing like their usual flight.

Juveniles (first year)

All three are brown above and streaked below with yellow eyes. Look at how the streaks are drawn, the patch under the tail, and the head.

Pitfalls

  • Juvenile goshawk vs. Cooper’s.The main trap. Check the undertail, the eyebrow, the tail bands and the bulk.
  • Streak pattern varies.Some Cooper’s are streaked further down the belly than usual. Combine streaks with head projection and tail shape.
  • Female Sharp-shinned vs. male Cooper’s.They are close in size, so size won’t settle it. Use head projection, tail tip and streaks.

Iowa timing

Fall counts from the Hitchcock Nature Center hawkwatch, Pottawattamie County (counts run about 1 September to mid-December):

  • Sharp-shinned: peaks 10 Oct 2020 and 30 Sep 2023; 75 on 12 Sep 2021.
  • Cooper’s: 1 Sep to 27 Nov in 2023, peak 11 Oct.
  • American Goshawk: 2–3 a year, from mid-October.

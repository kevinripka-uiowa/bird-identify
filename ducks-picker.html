    Notes and sources

    • Hybrids are common. Mallard crosses with all three. Warning signs: bold white bars on the speculum, a whitish tail, curled central tail feathers, or green on a male’s head. Mexican × Mottled hybrids occur in South Texas brush country, where the two meet.
    • Texas in January. Mottled Duck lives on the upper and central coast and has spread inland. Mexican Duck lives in the Trans-Pecos and along the border, east to about Webb County. American Black Duck is very rare; the Texas Bird Records Committee had accepted only 8 records by 2010. check current review status.
    • Iowa. Mottled Duck is listed as Casual (seen in 5–8 of the last 10 years) and every report needs documentation; many local claims are dark Mallards or hybrids. Mexican Duck has no Iowa record. American Black Duck is Regular.
    • Legs are orange in all four, brighter on males, so they don’t help.

    Sources: Ducks Unlimited species pages (Mottled, Mexican, Black); All About Birds species comparisons (Mexican, Mottled); Audubon field guide; Mexican × Mottled hybrid zone study (2024); Johnson & Garrettson 2010, Black Ducks in Texas; Iowa Ornithologists’ Union checklist.

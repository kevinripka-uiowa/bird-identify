Chestnut-collared, Lapland and Smith’s Longspurs

Chestnut-collared Longspur is a western prairie bird with 14 accepted Iowa records, 12 of them from 12 March to 23 April, mostly in the western counties. It hides in the Lapland and Smith’s flocks that move through Iowa in early spring. The tail pattern works in every plumage, and the flight call backs it up. Try the Longspur Picker to work through a bird in the field.

Who to expect

  • Lapland LongspurCommon in winterEarly September to mid-May, mostly November to March. Big flocks on plowed and stubble fields, often with Horned Larks.
  • Smith’s LongspurUncommon migrantMainly March to April in short grass; rarer in October and November.
  • Chestnut-collared LongspurAccidental14 accepted records, 1982–2009: 12 from 12 Mar to 23 Apr, peaking around 10–12 Apr, plus 9 Oct 2002 and 24 Oct 2009.

Behavior and pitfalls

  • Size.Chestnut-collared is the smallest of the three; Iowa’s first looked smaller than the Smith’s beside it.
  • Where.Iowa’s birds were in corn stubble and short grass with Lapland and Smith’s flocks. Smith’s wants short grass; Lapland takes plowed fields.
  • Flushed flocks.Smith’s rattle, circle and land some distance away. Watch the tails as birds land: the white Chestnut-collared tail stands out.
  • Thick-billed (McCown’s) Longspur.Not on the Iowa list, though three old reports were reviewed. Its tail shows a black inverted T, close to Chestnut-collared’s triangle, and it has a bigger bill.
  • Older records.Iowa has reports from 1887 to 1939 that aren’t in the accepted list.

Iowa timing

  • Lapland: fall from 6 Sep; spring until 21 May.
  • Smith’s: spring 25 Feb to 11 May; fall 28 Sep to 7 Jan.
  • Chestnut-collared: spring 12 Mar to 23 Apr; fall 9 and 24 Oct.

    Notes and sources

    • Eye, bill and tail first. A pale eye rules out Red-winged and cowbird; a cone bill means cowbird; a long, keeled tail means a grackle. Gloss only shows in good light.
    • Rusty vs. Brewer’s is the hard pair. In fall and winter, Rusty is edged rusty all over and Brewer’s is not. In spring, look at the female’s eye (pale on Rusty, usually dark on Brewer’s) and the male’s gloss (purple head on Brewer’s, dull on Rusty). Neither nests in Iowa.
    • Iowa. All six are regular. Red-winged, Common Grackle, cowbird and Great-tailed Grackle nest here; Great-tailed first nested in 1983 in Fremont County and has spread since. Rusty and Brewer’s are migrants, with extreme dates of 6 Sep–29 Apr (Rusty) and 26 Sep–11 May (Brewer’s). European Starling (short tail, yellow or spotted bill) and Yellow-headed Blackbird are the usual look-alikes.

    Sources: Cornell Lab, All About Birds species comparisons for Rusty, Brewer’s, Red-winged, Great-tailed Grackle, Common Grackle and Brown-headed Cowbird; USGS Patuxent ID pages for Rusty and Brewer’s; Iowa Ornithologists’ Union checklist and early and late dates; Silcock, Great-tailed Grackle range expansion.

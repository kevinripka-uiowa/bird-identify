Dusky Ducks: Mottled, Mexican, American Black and Mallard

Four brown, Mallard-shaped ducks: Mottled, Mexican and American Black Ducks, and female or eclipse male Mallards. Rule out Mallard with the speculum and tail first, then use the face, bill and body. All four hybridize, so some birds won’t fit any one species.

Who to expect

  • MallardCommon everywhereThe default brown duck in both states. A brown male Mallard is in eclipse, about June to September; by winter, drakes have green heads.
  • Mottled DuckTexas coastIowa: CasualResident on the upper and central Texas coast, spreading inland. In Iowa it’s listed as Casual (seen in 5–8 of the last 10 years) and every report needs documentation; first Iowa record 7 Jul 2006 at Saylorville.
  • Mexican DuckWest TexasResident in the Trans-Pecos and along the border, east to about Webb County, and spreading into central Texas. Meets Mottled Duck in South Texas brush country, where they hybridize. No Iowa record.
  • American Black DuckTexas: very rareIowa: RegularThe Texas Bird Records Committee had accepted only 8 records by 2010, over half from the coastal prairies.check In Iowa it’s Regular (seen in 7–10 of the last 10 years) and has nested.

Step 1: Rule out Mallard

Tap a check to see where to look. The small duck at top right shows the underwing in flight.

White in the speculum or tail means Mallard genes. A dark duck with bold white speculum bars or a pale tail is a Mallard or a hybrid, whatever its body color.

Step 2: The hard pairs

Mottled vs. Mexican Duck (Texas)

Both have dark tails and are darker than a female Mallard. Location narrows it: Mottled on the coast, Mexican in the west, both in South Texas brush country.

Pitfalls

  • They hybridize.A 2024 genetic study found a contact zone about 100 km wide in South Texas brush country, so birds there may be mixed.
  • Gulf Coast Mottled are dark.Western Gulf Coast birds are darker than Florida’s, closer to Mexican Duck in tone.

American Black Duck

The darkest of the four. In Iowa it’s the expected dark duck; in Texas it’s very rare, so a dark Mottled is far more likely there.

Pitfalls

  • Some Mottled look like Black Ducks.A Georgia eBird reviewer notes Mottled Ducks with dark caps and lores, streaked cheeks and red legs. Check the throat and the gape spot.
  • Black × Mallard hybrids are common.They are paler than a Black Duck, with a blue speculum and white on one or both edges.

Hybrids and domestic ducks

Mallard crosses with all three, and feral Mallards are common near towns. Look for Mallard traits on an otherwise dusky bird.

Pitfalls

  • Mexican × Mallard intergrades are widespreadin the Southwest; many West Texas birds show some Mallard traits.
  • Young male Mexican Ducks can show some green and curled tail featherswithout being hybrids (Ducks Unlimited).

    Notes and sources

    • Red-tailed forms in Iowa. Eastern is the resident breeder and Iowa’s commonest hawk. Northern (abieticola) comes in winter, with a dark head and heavy belly band. Krider’s is a pale prairie form, Western (calurus) a rare migrant and winter bird with light, rufous and dark morphs, and Harlan’s a rare winter bird, mostly blackish with a whitish or gray mottled tail. Northern and Krider’s are debated as true subspecies, and many birds are intergrades. check No Iowa source gives seasons or numbers for these forms.
    • Dark birds. Dark Western Red-tailed, Harlan’s, Swainson’s, Rough-legged and Ferruginous all have dark morphs; Broad-winged rarely does. Judge the tail and the underwing flight feathers, not the body.
    • Hard pairs. Juvenile Red-shouldered vs. Broad-winged: crescents vs. a square panel in the wing, even vs. tip-heavy tail bands, square vs. pointed wingtips.
    • Iowa status (Iowa Ornithologists’ Union checklist). Ferruginous is Casual, mostly western Iowa in fall. Rough-legged is a winter visitor from late October. Broad-winged peaks in late September; Swainson’s late September to early October; Red-tailed late October to early November.

    Sources: HawkWatch International raptor ID fact sheets; Hitchcock Nature Center hawkwatch reports (2020, 2021, 2023); Iowa Ornithologists’ Union checklist; Iowa DNR raptor guide; Harlan’s Hawk overview.

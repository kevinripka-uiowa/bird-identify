    Notes and sources

    • Use several marks. Every single mark has exceptions. Voice, the eye-to-bill connection and the yellow spot carry the most weight; size only works with the other species alongside.
    • Young birds are harder. Bill color varies between young swans of the same species and age, and young Tundras can show a V forehead. Plumage by date and leg color help most.
    • Iowa. Trumpeters were restored here from 1995 on; the DNR counted a record 158 nests in 2025, and over 4,700 swans were tallied in the 2025 midwinter survey. Tundras pass through in spring and fall, many along the Mississippi River. Mute Swans (orange bill with a black knob) are feral escapes.

    Sources: Deviche and Jenness, “Identification challenge: Trumpeter vs. Tundra Swan,” Arizona Birds 16:13–22 (2022); David Sibley, Distinguishing Trumpeter and Tundra Swans; Southern Wisconsin Bird Alliance, Tundra or Trumpeter?; Iowa DNR, Trumpeter Swans and the 30 Dec 2025 news release; USGS, Tundra Swans on the Upper Mississippi.
